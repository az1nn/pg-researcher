# PG Religion Research — Starter Bibliography

This is the first controlled bibliography for the Hermetic/Pythagorean core. It separates **research authority** from **publication rights**: a source can be excellent for scholarship while remaining quotation-only or research-only for e-book production.

## Hermetic core

### Brian P. Copenhaver — *Hermetica: The Greek Corpus Hermeticum and the Latin Asclepius in a New English Translation, with Notes and Introduction*

- Role: primary modern critical translation and scholarly orientation.
- Publisher: Cambridge University Press.
- Print publication: 1992.
- Corpus covered: Corpus Hermeticum plus Latin *Asclepius*.
- PG class: `academic_critical` + translation of primary material.
- Rights posture: `quotation_only` / `research_only` until edition-specific permissions are established.
- Use: historical context, terminology, textual comparison and checking claims derived from older public-domain translations.
- Important: do not treat this modern translation as public domain merely because the underlying Hermetic texts are ancient.

Canonical reference: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/hermetica/144186FB1881CC2F9A9153A53B85B38D

### G. R. S. Mead — *Thrice-Greatest Hermes* (1906)

- Role: historically influential English-language Hermetic scholarship/translation.
- PG class: `modern_esoteric` / historical scholarship.
- Rights posture: likely public-domain original edition in many jurisdictions, but the exact scan/transcription must be verified before publication reuse.
- Use: compare the history of Hermetic reception and provide a potentially reusable public-domain textual layer after verification.
- Caveat: older scholarship is not automatically preferred over modern philology.

## Pythagorean core

### Carl A. Huffman — “Pythagoreanism”, *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*

- Role: contemporary scholarly orientation and source criticism.
- Substantive revision: 2024.
- PG class: `academic_critical`.
- Rights posture: `research_only` / brief attributed quotation.
- Use: distinguish historical Pythagoras, early Pythagoreans and later Neopythagorean constructions.
- Key methodological consequence: later claims about Pythagoras must not automatically be projected back onto the historical figure.

Canonical reference: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pythagoreanism/

### Carl A. Huffman — “Pythagoras”, *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*

- Role: source-critical biography and reconstruction of the historical Pythagoras.
- PG class: `academic_critical`.
- Rights posture: `research_only` / brief attributed quotation.
- Use: gate claims about mathematics, cosmology, metempsychosis, ritual and the Pythagorean way of life.

Canonical reference: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pythagoras/

### Iamblichus — *Life of Pythagoras* / Thomas Taylor translation (1818)

- Role: late antique Pythagorean/Neoplatonic reception; not a contemporary biography of Pythagoras.
- PG class: `tradition_commentary` / ancient reception source.
- Rights posture: the 1818 Taylor translation is a candidate for public-domain use, subject to verification of the exact edition/transcription.
- Use: study initiatory discipline, symbolic prescriptions and the late construction of Pythagorean authority.
- Caveat: separate Iamblichus' fourth-century CE testimony from evidence for the sixth/fifth-century BCE historical Pythagoras.

### *The Golden Verses of Pythagoras* — historical English translations

- Role: ethical/Pythagorean reception text.
- PG class: `tradition_commentary` / attributed Pythagorean material.
- Rights posture: old translations (e.g. Hall, Dacier-derived, Taylor) may provide public-domain candidates after edition verification.
- Use: ethical discipline, self-examination, order and practice.
- Caveat: attribution and dating must remain explicit; do not present the verses as a securely authored work by the historical Pythagoras.

## First methodological finding

The Pythagorean track needs an especially strict **chronology gate**. Modern scholarship emphasizes that Pythagoras wrote nothing and that later ancient sources often magnified him into the originator of doctrines developed centuries later. For PG Religion Research, every Pythagorean claim should therefore carry both `source_date/context` and `claim_type` before it can enter a comparative e-book.

## Initial e-book-safe strategy

1. Use current scholarship (Copenhaver/Huffman and comparable academic work) to establish history and terminology.
2. Use verified public-domain editions only where we need larger quoted primary-text passages.
3. Paraphrase protected modern scholarship and cite it; never build a derivative substitute for a protected edition.
4. Generate original PG synthesis from multiple independently tracked claims rather than rewriting one modern author.
5. Keep Hermetic, historical Pythagorean and Neopythagorean layers distinct before drawing correspondences.
