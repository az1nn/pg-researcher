# PG RELIGION RESEARCH

## Mission

**PG RELIGION RESEARCH** is the research and publishing arm used to transform religious, philosophical, esoteric and popular-wisdom sources into rigorous, accessible and visually authored Prince' Gutt e-books.

The project searches for concepts with broad human resonance while preserving the historical identity of each tradition.

Its strongest comparative lenses are Hermetic and Pythagorean, but those lenses are interpretive tools rather than permission to force every source into Hermeticism or Pythagoreanism.

## The core rule

> Compare without erasing difference.

A recurring idea can be valuable even when traditions disagree about its meaning, metaphysics, ritual use or historical origin.

We therefore separate five layers in every publication:

| Layer | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Source | What does the text/source actually say? |
| Context | When, where and within what tradition does it appear? |
| Interpretation | How is it understood by scholars or practitioners? |
| Correspondence | What defensible parallel can be drawn to another tradition? |
| PG synthesis | What original teaching/editorial frame do we build from the comparison? |

## Editorial voice

The writing should be clear enough for a reader with no academic background while retaining source discipline.

Desired qualities:

- concise, serious and invitational;
- symbolic without becoming vague;
- philosophical without pretending speculation is history;
- spiritually open without demanding assent;
- contemporary Portuguese with important original-language terms explained when useful;
- Prince' Gutt presence through authorship, framing, design and voice rather than artificial self-insertion into every paragraph.

## Universalism

The project uses **universalist** to describe a comparative search for recurring human questions and patterns.

It does not claim that all religions are identical, that one hidden institution authored them all, or that resemblance by itself proves direct historical transmission.

Where a publication advances a perennialist, occult or initiatory interpretation, that interpretation must be labeled and sourced to the relevant author or to PG Religion Research.

## Research packet per concept

Before manuscript writing, each concept receives a research packet:

```text
concept/
├── brief.md
├── sources.yaml
├── evidence/
├── claims/
├── parallels.yaml
├── rights.yaml
├── outline.md
└── source-ledger.md
```

Minimum questions:

1. What is the exact concept?
2. Which source is primary?
3. What is the earliest defensible textual/historical context?
4. Which translation/edition is being used and what are its rights?
5. Which interpretations are scholarly and which are tradition-internal?
6. Which parallels are structural, lexical, historical or only editorial?
7. What does the Hermetic lens add?
8. What does the Pythagorean lens add?
9. What should explicitly *not* be claimed?
10. What original Prince' Gutt synthesis is worth publishing?

## E-book production pipeline

```text
Discovery
  -> Source registry
  -> Rights classification
  -> Evidence capture
  -> Atomic claims
  -> Historical/context review
  -> Comparative mapping
  -> PG editorial thesis
  -> Outline
  -> Manuscript
  -> Citation/rights audit
  -> Visual direction
  -> PDF/ePub build
  -> Human editorial gate
  -> Release archive
```

No stage may erase the provenance of the previous stage.

## Manuscript anatomy

Recommended structure for short-to-medium e-books:

1. **Cover / title** — Prince' Gutt identity, restrained symbolism.
2. **Opening thesis** — the question the volume answers.
3. **How to read this book** — source/synthesis distinction.
4. **Origin** — historical and textual context.
5. **The concept** — primary teaching in its own tradition.
6. **Correspondences** — carefully qualified comparisons.
7. **Hermetic bridge** — only where textually/conceptually defensible.
8. **Pythagorean bridge** — number, harmony, proportion, cosmos or discipline where relevant.
9. **Prince' Gutt synthesis** — original contemporary reading.
10. **Practice/reflection** — contemplative questions, journaling, observation or study exercises; never fabricated ritual authority.
11. **Notes and sources**.
12. **Rights / edition note**.

## Design doctrine

The religious line inherits Royal Street rather than adopting generic New Age aesthetics.

**Palette and material:** black, burgundy, aged gold, warm paper, metal, wood and film grain.

**Composition:** sparse, editorial, high contrast, strong margins, real texture, few symbols.

**Photography:** authorized/real Prince' Gutt material is the human anchor when the artist appears.

**Symbol use:** diagrams, seals, alphabets, geometry and religious symbols must have source/context notes. Decorative invention must not be presented as an ancient symbol.

**Avoid:** neon violet mysticism, glossy AI temples, random zodiac collages, fake parchment, excessive sacred geometry, pseudo-Latin and synthetic manuscript pages.

## Rights doctrine

The project has two different rights questions:

1. **Prince' Gutt assets** — handled under the existing artist authorization/provenance rules.
2. **Books/scriptures/artworks** — handled per work, edition, translation, jurisdiction and intended use.

A text being ancient does not make a recent translation public domain.

For protected works, extract ideas and cite/paraphrase. Do not build a substitute edition of the source.

## Initial research map

The first collection should prioritize concepts that let the methodology prove itself:

| Track | Initial question |
| --- | --- |
| Hermetic | What do *Corpus Hermeticum* and later Hermetic reception actually teach about mind, cosmos and ascent? |
| Pythagorean | How do number, harmony, proportion and ethical discipline relate in Pythagorean traditions? |
| Comparative | What does reciprocity/"Golden Rule" look like across several traditions without claiming identity? |
| Logos | How do word, reason and cosmic order differ across Greek philosophy, Hermetic material and Christian usage? |
| Measure | How do number, proportion and sacred order appear in philosophy, architecture and ritual symbolism? |
| Silence | Why do contemplative and initiatory traditions repeatedly value silence, and where do their meanings diverge? |
